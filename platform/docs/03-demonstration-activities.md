# 3. Demonstration activities

Three complete activities. All materials are original and fictional. Intentionally flawed answers and synthetic data are labelled as teaching material wherever students see them.

| Activity | Module | Status |
|---|---|---|
| A. Evidence checking | 4 | **Built end to end**: public sample at `/try` and assignable lesson |
| B. Business analysis | 5 | **Built** as the Module 5 lesson |
| C. Prompt improvement | 2 | **Built** as the Module 2 lesson, with both prompt versions kept |

Activity A is the stand-alone public demonstration. B and C are built as course lessons.

---

## A. Evidence checking (built)

**Scenario.** A council committee member asked an AI assistant whether the (fictional) Harbourview Library should open on Sundays. The answer is about to be forwarded. Students check it against three supplied sources, correct it and say what remains uncertain.

**Materials.** Source A (library annual report excerpt), Source B (self-selected resident survey), Source C (budget note). Full text is in `src/content/lessons.js` and on `/curriculum/sample-lesson`.

**The flawed answer** (written by the course authors, not generated by a model):

| Claim | Planted problem | Correct verdict |
|---|---|---|
| C1: 290 visits per Sunday; about a third new visitors | None (41 of 118 = 35%) | Supported (A) |
| C2: trial "caused" a 16% rise in memberships | Wrong number (6%) and unsupported causation | Contradicted (A) |
| C3: "nearly two-thirds of residents" support it | 61% of self-selected respondents ≠ residents; "overwhelmingly" overstates | Partly supported (B) |
| C4: mainly attracts families with young children | Plausible, unsupported | Not found in the sources |
| C5: "extending weekend hours typically increases use by around 20% (Marlow & Achebe, 2021, *Journal of Public Library Management*)" | **Invented reference** and its figure | Reference could not be verified |
| C6: "minimal cost by using existing staff" | Contradicted: £74,000 a year, voluntary shifts, union not agreed | Contradicted (C) |
| C7 (independent): "Saturday is the quietest day" | Saturday is the busiest | Contradicted (A) |

**Required outputs.** A verdict with source and evidence note for each claim; a corrected answer using only checked claims; an uncertainty statement; the independent check.

**What remains uncertain (from the example answer).** Whether trial attendance would last beyond eight weeks; whether respondents represent residents generally; how many Sunday visits would be new rather than moved from Saturday; staff availability; which funding option the council would choose.

**Automated provisional feedback** shows at most two issues, in this priority order: unanswered claims; accepting the invented reference; keeping the invented reference or its figure in the answer; missing the cost contradiction; misstating the membership figure or causation; over-generalising the survey; accepting the unsupported claim; no corrected answer or cost left out; no uncertainty statement; evidence not recorded.

## B. Business analysis (built as Module 5)

**Scenario.** A (fictional) regional sales manager writes: *"Which region is our top performer this half-year? I need a recommendation for where to put our extra sales rep next quarter."*

**Materials.** [`materials/module5-sales-synthetic.csv`](materials/module5-sales-synthetic.csv), eight rows, labelled as synthetic:

| quarter | region | units_sold | gross_revenue_gbp | returns_gbp |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2025-Q1 | North | 1200 | 30000 | 900 |
| 2025-Q2 | North | 1260 | 31500 | 1100 |
| 2025-Q1 | South | 950 | 23750 | 400 |
| 2025-Q2 | South | 1140 | 28500 | 450 |
| 2025-Q1 | East | 1300 | 32500 | 3900 |
| 2025-Q2 | East | 1330 | *(missing)* | 4200 |
| 2025-Q1 | West | 800 | 20000 | 200 |
| 2025-Q2 | West | 1000 | 25000 | 250 |

**Planted issues.**
1. **Missing value:** East Q2 revenue is blank. Every other row has a unit price of exactly £25, so it can reasonably be estimated as 1,330 × £25 = £33,250, as long as the assumption is stated. Treating it as zero is wrong.
2. **Ambiguous definition:** "top performer" could mean gross revenue, net revenue after returns, or growth. The manager's purpose (where to add a rep) matters.
3. **Returns:** East has a returns rate of about 12%, far above the other regions.

**Answer key** (computed by script from the CSV; East Q2 estimated at £33,250):

| Region | Gross H1 | Net H1 (after returns) | Returns rate | Growth Q1→Q2 (gross) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| North | £61,500 | **£59,500** | 3.3% | 5.0% |
| South | £52,250 | £51,400 | 1.6% | 20.0% |
| East | **£65,750** | £57,650 | 12.3% | 2.3% |
| West | £45,000 | £44,550 | 1.0% | **25.0%** |

So the "top" region is East by gross revenue, North by net revenue and West by growth. If the blank is treated as zero, East appears to collapse (gross £32,500, growth −100%). That is the error to catch.

**Student steps.**
1. Clarify the objective: write the question you would ask the manager, and state the definition you will use and why.
2. Inspect the data: find the missing value and the returns pattern before calculating anything.
3. Choose an analysis and ask AI for a spreadsheet formula or short script if permitted.
4. Check: recalculate at least one region by hand and test the formula on that row.
5. Write a recommendation of no more than 150 words with limitations: the estimated value, two quarters only, and no data on costs or rep capacity.

**Example response (abridged).** "I asked whether the rep should go where sales are growing or where they are largest. Assuming growth matters most for a new rep, West grew fastest (25% quarter on quarter) from the smallest base, and South (20%) is close behind. East's Q2 revenue is missing; I estimated it at £33,250 from the constant £25 unit price. Even so, East has the highest gross revenue but a 12% returns rate, which should be investigated before adding sales effort there. Limitations: two quarters only, one estimated value, and no information on rep capacity or margins."

**Common mistakes.** Treating the blank as zero; answering "East" or "North" without stating the definition; trusting an AI-written formula without testing it on a known row; ignoring returns.

**Rubric.** *Task definition* (objective and definition clarified); *Verification* (missing value handled and disclosed; independent recalculation matches); *Responsible use* (the assumption is stated in the recommendation).

## C. Prompt improvement (built as Module 2)

**Scenario.** A deliberately vague tutor exercise, *"Write something about how small businesses can use social media"*, and a source pack about a fictional bakery (P1 profile, P2 owner's notes, P3 marking guidance).

**Student steps.**
1. Write a brief: objective, audience, inputs, constraints, output format and checkable success criteria.
2. Write prompt v1 and run it with the approved model, or record output from an approved tool with its name.
3. Evaluate the output against each success criterion (met, partly met or not met).
4. Change **one** aspect, linked to a criterion that was not met, and run prompt v2.
5. Judge whether the change helped, citing the criterion and the evidence, and say how confident you can be from one output.

**Preservation of both attempts.** Each run is stored separately with its prompt, output, requested and served model identifiers, settings and timestamp, and is labelled "prompt v1" or "prompt v2". Resubmissions create new versions; earlier versions are never overwritten.

**Rubric view.** The marking guidance (P3) penalises generic tips, so a prompt that omits the owner's constraints (three hours a week, no paid ads, no filming, the goal is cake orders) fails on substance however long it is. Automated feedback checks whether those constraints and the client inputs reach the prompt, whether the criteria are checkable and whether the judgement cites evidence. It deliberately does not reward length or vocabulary.

**Teaching point on comparison.** Hold materials and model constant, record the model, and don't treat one better output as proof that the new prompt is reliably better. Where time allows, run each version twice.
